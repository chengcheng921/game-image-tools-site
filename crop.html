How to Use the Image Cropper

  1. Upload — Drag a screenshot onto the drop zone or click to browse. PNG, JPG, and WebP up to 20MB work.
  2. Draw the crop box — Drag anywhere on the image to create a selection. Drag the round handles to resize it, or drag inside the box to reposition.
  3. Pick an aspect ratio — Choose Free for any shape, or lock to 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 3:2, or 9:16 and the box keeps that proportion as you resize.
  4. Crop, check, download — Click Crop to see the exact result with its dimensions and file size. Not happy? Click Back to Edit. Happy? Download as PNG, JPEG, or WebP.

Crop Screenshots to Kill HUDs, Taskbars, and Clutter

Great screenshots are ruined by noisy edges: the Windows taskbar, a chat overlay, party frames, or a minimap pulling focus from the action. Cropping tight around your subject instantly makes captures look intentional. It's also the fastest way to reframe a widescreen shot for a platform with a different shape — cut a 16:9 gameplay moment down to a square avatar or a vertical 9:16 short without opening a heavyweight editor.

Which Aspect Ratio Should I Choose?

Match the ratio to the destination: 16:9 for YouTube thumbnails and Steam capsules, 1:1 for avatars and profile pictures, 9:16 for TikTok/Shorts, and 4:3 or 3:2 for classic photo prints and blog headers. When in doubt, crop in Free mode and check the pixel dimensions shown on the result screen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does cropping reduce image quality?

The pixels you keep are preserved exactly. The result is re-encoded when you download — choose PNG for lossless output, or JPEG/WebP for a smaller file.

Can I adjust the crop after clicking Crop?

Yes — click Back to Edit to return to the full image and adjust your selection. Nothing is final until you download.

Is there a file size limit?

Images up to 20MB are supported, and everything is processed locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded, no account needed.
