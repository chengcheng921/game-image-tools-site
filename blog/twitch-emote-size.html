Twitch Emote Size: 112×112, 56×56 and 28×28

Published on Nov 14, 2026 · 5 min read

If you only remember one number, remember 112 × 112. That is the size Twitch asks for on upload and the largest size your emote will ever be drawn at. The 56 × 56 and 28 × 28 versions are what viewers actually see depending on their screen density, which is why an emote that looks crisp at full size can turn to mush in chat. Below are the exact specs, the two upload routes Twitch offers today, the review rules that get emotes rejected, and a short workflow for producing a clean 112 × 112 PNG from artwork you already have.

Twitch Emote Specs at a Glance

The 1:1 rule catches more first-time uploaders than any other. Chat reserves a square box per emote, so a wide banner or a tall character portrait cannot be displayed without cropping it. Design inside a square from the start rather than making a horizontal image and hoping it survives.

Why Three Sizes Exist

Browsers pick an image based on how many physical pixels each CSS pixel occupies. On a phone or a high-DPI monitor, chat requests the 3x asset and draws it at 112 × 112. On an older 1080p display with chat at default zoom, it may only need the 1x asset at 28 × 28. Twitch serves the closest match so that chat stays sharp without downloading oversized files for everyone.

The practical consequence: 28 × 28 is not a thumbnail you can ignore. It is the only version a large share of viewers see. A 1-pixel outline, a pair of tiny eyes or a three-word caption disappears entirely at that size, while a bold shape with two or three colors stays readable. Design for 28 first and scale up, not the other way around.

The Two Upload Routes (Most Guides Are Out of Date)

Older tutorials state flatly that you must export and upload three separate files for every emote. That was true for years and is no longer the whole picture. Twitch's emote uploader now supports two paths:

  • Auto-resize: upload one square PNG between 112 × 112 and 4096 × 4096 px, under 1 MB. Twitch generates the 28, 56 and 112 pixel versions itself.
  • Manual: upload three files — 112 × 112, 56 × 56 and 28 × 28 — each a PNG under 1 MB.

Open your emote dashboard and see which one you get. If there is a single upload field with a size range, you are on the auto-resize route and only need one file. If there are three slots, export all three from the same master. Either way, never take a 28 × 28 file and enlarge it to 112 × 112 to satisfy the uploader: the result is blocky, and reviewers reject visibly upscaled art.

If you do export manually, downscale from the largest file rather than drawing each size by hand. A 112 pixel master resized down to 56 and 28 keeps the same silhouette across all three densities, so the emote does not appear to change shape as viewers scroll between devices.

What Gets an Emote Rejected

Twitch reviews every sub emote before it becomes usable, and the review queue is where optimistic uploads die. The technical rejects are easy to avoid: not square, a solid background, a file over 1 MB, or a format other than PNG. The content rejects are the ones people miss:

  • Real people. Photographs of yourself, friends or streamers are not accepted, even heavily stylized ones.
  • Copyrighted characters. Existing game characters, anime characters and brand mascots are rejected for infringement.
  • Hate symbols, sexual content, gore and graphic violence. These fall under Twitch's community guidelines and are removed on sight.
  • Advertising. An emote built around a sponsor logo or a URL is treated as promotion, not expression.
  • Illegible text. A single character can work, but a word or slogan is unreadable at 28 pixels and regularly comes back as too detailed.

Review also considers quality, not just policy. A low-effort resize of a screenshot, or an emote with a visible white fringe around the cutout, can be sent back even when it breaks no rule.

How to Produce a Clean 112 × 112 Emote

This takes about five minutes and works with any source art — a drawing, a logo, or a cropped frame from your overlay.

  • Step 1. Start from a square master at 512 × 512 or larger. If your artwork is not square, open the image cropper, switch to a 1:1 preset and frame the subject with about 10% empty space on every side. That padding stops the outline from touching the chat edge.
  • Step 2. Resize to 112 × 112 with the image resizer. Enable "Keep aspect ratio" so the square stays square, and type the exact dimensions rather than dragging a corner.
  • Step 3. Check the alpha channel. Place the emote on a white background and on a dark one; any grey halo or leftover matte means the cutout is dirty and should be redone.
  • Step 4. Export as PNG. A 112 pixel emote is typically a few kilobytes, so the 1 MB ceiling is only a concern if you are uploading a 4096 pixel master for auto-resize.
  • Step 5. If you need the 56 and 28 pixel exports, resize from the 112 file rather than the 512 master so the anti-aliasing matches. Use the resizer twice and keep the output as PNG.
  • Step 6. Shrink the preview to 28 × 28 on screen and look at it from a normal viewing distance. If you cannot tell what it is within a second, simplify the design before uploading.

The 28-Pixel Legibility Checklist

Most rejected-for-quality emotes are not badly drawn; they are drawn for the wrong size. Run through this list before you upload:

How Many Emotes You Can Upload

Affiliates start with five sub emote slots and unlock additional slots as subscriber points accumulate. Partners begin with a larger allowance and can reach 60 slots at the top of the ladder. Because slot thresholds and totals have changed several times, treat any fixed number in a blog post as approximate and check the Emote page of your Creator Dashboard for your current allowance.

Each slot holds one emote at a time, and swapping in a new design means going back through review. That is another argument for getting the 28 pixel version right on the first attempt rather than iterating after approval.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size should a Twitch emote be?

Upload at 112 by 112 pixels. Twitch displays every emote at three densities: 112 by 112, 56 by 56 and 28 by 28. The file must be a square PNG with a transparent background and no larger than 1 MB.

Do I have to upload three separate files for one emote?

Not necessarily. Twitch's uploader can take a single square PNG between 112 and 4096 pixels and generate the three display sizes for you. If your dashboard asks for three separate files instead, supply 112 by 112, 56 by 56 and 28 by 28 exports of the same artwork.

Can a Twitch emote have a white or colored background?

No. Emotes are drawn directly onto chat, which can be light, dark or a custom channel theme, so the artwork must be a transparent PNG. A baked-in background shows up as a visible square and is a common reason for rejection.

Why does my emote look blurry or muddy in chat?

Two causes dominate. First, the emote was upscaled from a smaller file instead of drawn at 112 pixels or above. Second, the design carries detail that only survives at full size, such as thin strokes, small text or soft gradients, all of which collapse at 28 pixels.

What is the maximum Twitch emote file size?

1 MB per file for static PNG emotes, and 1 MB for animated emotes. A 112 by 112 PNG is normally only a few kilobytes, so the limit mainly matters when you upload a large square master and let Twitch downscale it.

How many emotes can I upload as an Affiliate?

Affiliates start with five sub emote slots and unlock more as subscriber points accumulate. Partners start with a larger allowance and can unlock up to 60 slots. Slot counts change over time, so check the Emote section of your Creator Dashboard for your current total.
