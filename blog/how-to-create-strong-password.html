How to Create a Strong Password (That Actually Protects You)

Published on Nov 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Your game accounts hold payment cards, rare skins, and years of progress — and most people protect them with a password a cracking rig guesses in minutes. The good news: real password strength is simple once you ignore the outdated advice. Here's what actually works in 2026.

Myth 1: "Complex" Substitutions Make Passwords Strong

P@ssw0rd! looks clever. To a cracking tool, it's the word "password" with a leetspeak filter — one of the first million guesses it tries. Attackers' dictionaries include every common substitution pattern, every keyboard walk (qwerty, 1qaz2wsx), and every "add 123!" trick. Human-invented complexity is predictable complexity.

What Actually Matters: Length and Randomness

Password strength is math: possible combinations = (character set size) ^ (length). A random 8-character password with all character types has about 95^8 combinations — crackable offline in days. A random 20-character password has 95^20, which is more combinations than there are grains of sand on Earth. Length multiplies; cleverness doesn't.

The second rule is uniqueness. When (not if) one site gets breached, attackers try your email+password pair everywhere else within hours. One reused password turns a forum leak into a drained bank account. Every account needs its own password — which means a password manager, because nobody memorizes 80 unique random strings.

Generating a Strong Password in 10 Seconds

  1. Open the password generator. It runs entirely in your browser tab — nothing you generate is sent anywhere, ever.
  2. Set the length to at least 16. For email, banking, and game accounts with saved payment info, go 20+.
  3. Keep all four character types on: uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols. More variety = more entropy per character.
  4. Click Generate and check the strength meter — you want "strong".
  5. Copy it straight into your password manager's entry for the site.

Why Local Generation Matters

Here's an uncomfortable truth about some online password generators: the password is created on their server and sent to you. If that server logs it — or gets compromised — your "random" password isn't yours alone. This generator runs 100% client-side and uses crypto.getRandomValues, the browser's cryptographically secure random source (not the predictable Math.random). You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it still works — that's the test of a trustworthy generator.

When a Site Rejects Your Symbols

Some ancient systems refuse <, >, or other special characters. Don't weaken everything else — just uncheck Symbols and bump the length to 24+. A long alphanumeric password beats a short symbol-heavy one anyway.

The Full Checklist

  • 16+ characters, randomly generated (not invented by a human)
  • Unique per account — a password manager makes this painless
  • Two-factor authentication on email and anything with payment info
  • Never share passwords over Discord/email — legit support never asks

Your future self — the one whose account didn't get stolen — says thanks.
