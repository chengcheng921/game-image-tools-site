Convert TXT to EPUB Free — Turn Notes Into Real Ebooks

Published on Nov 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Plain text files are where good writing goes to die. No chapters, no bookmarks, no resizable fonts — just an endless wall of characters that's misery to read on a phone or e-reader. Converting to EPUB fixes all of that in about ten seconds, and you don't need Calibre or any software install to do it.

Why Bother With EPUB at All?

EPUB is the open ebook standard that everything except old Kindles reads natively: Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play Books, and every serious e-reader app. An EPUB remembers your place, reflows text to any screen size, and gives you a tappable table of contents. Use cases that come up constantly:

  • Writers: proofread your Markdown or TXT draft on a real e-reader — typos jump out in book form.
  • Gamers: turn a 100KB text walkthrough or fan-translated guide into couch-readable form.
  • Students: convert exported notes into something you can actually study from on the bus.

Step 1: Open the Converter

Head to the free EPUB converter. The whole conversion runs in your browser — your manuscript is never uploaded, which matters a lot for unpublished drafts.

Step 2: Upload Your File

Drop in a TXT, HTML, Markdown (.md), or PDF file up to 20MB. The title field auto-fills from the filename — edit it and add your author name so the ebook's metadata looks right in your library.

Step 3: Let Language Detection Do Its Thing

The tool detects English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean content automatically and tags the EPUB accordingly. This isn't translation — it tells the reader app which fonts, line-breaking rules, and text-to-speech voice to use.

Step 4: Convert and Download

Click Convert to EPUB and the file downloads immediately. Open it in Apple Books, Kobo, or your reader of choice and check the table of contents.

How Chapters Are Created

This is the part most converters get wrong. Ours works in two modes:

  • Headings become chapters. If your document uses Markdown #/## headings or HTML H1/H2 tags, each becomes a chapter entry in the table of contents.
  • Plain TXT gets auto-chunked. No headings at all? The text is split into chapters of roughly 50 paragraphs each, so navigation still works instead of dumping you into one 400-page scroll.

Tip: if you're converting a TXT guide, spend two minutes adding # headings in a text editor first — the resulting table of contents is worth it.

What About Kindle?

Modern Kindles accept EPUB through Amazon's Send to Kindle service — just email the file or use the app, and Amazon converts it automatically. Older devices can go through Calibre's EPUB-to-MOBI/AZW3 conversion.

A Note on PDF Sources

PDF conversion extracts the text. Images and complex multi-column layouts don't carry over — that's a limitation of PDF as a source format everywhere, not just here. For text-heavy PDFs (exported articles, transcripts, manuals) it works beautifully.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my formatting survive the conversion?

Plain TXT has no formatting to preserve. Paragraph breaks are kept. If your notes use Markdown headers, italics or lists, convert from Markdown instead — the EPUB will render the styling properly.

Can I convert in bulk?

Yes. Drop multiple TXT files into the converter at once; each becomes a separate EPUB. For chapters of one book, merge them into a single file first with clear section breaks.

Do I need an account or email?

No. The converter runs locally in your browser. No upload, no sign-up, no watermark on the resulting EPUB.
