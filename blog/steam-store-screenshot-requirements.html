Steam Store Screenshot Requirements: 1920×1080 and Review Rules

Published on Nov 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Steam publishes the technical rules for store screenshots, but most rejections come from habits rather than numbers: capturing at the desktop's aspect ratio, leaving a chat overlay in frame, or baking a launch discount into the image. The fix is a repeatable capture and edit routine. This guide covers what Steamworks actually requires, where each screenshot is rendered, what review commonly flags, and how to turn raw captures into a clean 1920×1080 gallery without uploading your art to a stranger's server.

Steam Store Screenshot Requirements

These are the constraints that matter before review even starts. The resolution and ratio rules are enforced by the uploader; the content rules are enforced by a human looking at your store page.

PropertyRequirementWhat it means in practice

Aspect ratio16:9Capture natively in 16:9; ultrawide or 16:10 frames show bars or get cropped

ContentActual gameplayConcept art and engine renders belong in other store sections
OverlaysNoneNo chat, FPS counters, debug text, watermarks, URLs, prices or review scores

The count is a minimum, not a target. Five screenshots can satisfy the uploader and still tell a player almost nothing. A store page needs to answer three questions quickly: what the game looks like in motion, what the player does, and why this session will differ from the last one. Choose frames that answer those separately instead of five variations of the same firefight.

Where Each Screenshot Appears

The first image is the one that gets reused most. Steam shows it in hover previews and compact modules where the frame may be only a few hundred pixels wide. A wide establishing shot with a tiny character reads as empty scenery at that size. A clear subject, strong contrast and a readable silhouette survive the shrink, which is why the opening frame should be composed like a thumbnail rather than a wallpaper.

The rest of the gallery is browsed deliberately, so it can carry more detail: a UI-heavy management screen, a wide environment, a close-up of a character or creature, and one frame that shows the game's tone rather than its systems. Avoid putting all the text-heavy interfaces in a row. Players scan thumbnails first, and five near-identical menus blur into one.

Why 1080p Is the Floor, Not the Ceiling

A 1280 × 720 capture stretched to 1920 × 1080 is one of the easiest ways to fail a quality check. The upscale cannot invent detail, so edges soften and fine UI text smears. Capture at 1920 × 1080 at minimum, and prefer 2560 × 1440 or 4K when the game and your GPU allow it. Downscaling a 4K frame to 1080p averages four pixels into one, which hides aliasing and compression noise and often looks sharper than a native 1080p grab.

Do not add letterboxing or pillarboxing to fit 16:9. If the game renders at 21:9, capture at a 16:9 resolution instead of padding the frame with black bars. Bars read as an unfinished asset and waste the most valuable part of the image.

What Store Review Commonly Flags

Review standards are applied to the store page as a whole, so a single bad frame can hold up the set. The recurring problems are:

  • Promotional text baked into the image. Launch dates, discount percentages, "wishlist now", review quotes, URLs and QR codes belong in the description or on a capsule, not inside a gameplay frame.
  • Non-gameplay material. Concept art, cinematic renders and logo cards presented as screenshots misrepresent the product.
  • Third-party marks. Unlicensed logos, brands, music player overlays or another game's footage.
  • Debug and placeholder content. Developer consoles, "WIP" labels, untextured assets and visible development menus.
  • Bars and mismatched ratios. Black borders, stretched images or a gallery that mixes 16:9 with ultrawide crops.
  • Low-quality upscales. Soft, blocky frames that were enlarged from a smaller capture.

If a frame needs an arrow and three sentences to explain it, it is probably a description problem rather than a screenshot problem. Pick a frame that shows the mechanic working.

Capture Setup That Avoids Rework

Most cleanup disappears if the capture is clean. Before taking a single screenshot:

  • Set the game to a native 16:9 resolution — 1920 × 1080, 2560 × 1440 or 3840 × 2160.
  • Use the in-game screenshot key, photo mode or platform capture. Avoid OS window captures that include borders, the taskbar or a second monitor.
  • Disable HUD and subtitles if the game allows it. If the HUD is part of the experience, compose the shot so it does not cover the subject.
  • Turn off Discord, Steam, GPU and capture-card overlays, plus notifications.
  • Capture the shipping build, not a debug build with console text or performance graphs.
  • Take more frames than you need and choose later; deleting is cheaper than reshooting.

Editing Raw Captures Into a 1080p Gallery

Once the frames are captured, the edit is short and should be identical for every image so the gallery feels consistent.

  1. Crop to 16:9. Use the browser-based crop tool to remove any border, stray UI or dead space. Crop before resizing so the subject is not squeezed.
  2. Resize to 1920 × 1080. The image resizer includes a 1080p preset. If you captured at 4K, resize down rather than letting Steam do it.
  3. Inspect at 100%. Check text, thin lines and the edges of the HUD. If the frame is soft, go back to the original capture instead of sharpening an upscale.
  4. Choose a format deliberately. PNG for menus and text-heavy UI, JPG at high quality for photoreal scenes. The format converter shows the size difference before you commit.
  5. Keep the order intentional. Name the files so the opening frame is obvious, and upload them in the order you want players to browse.

File Size and Double Compression

Steam re-encodes the images it serves, so upload a clean master rather than a file that has already been through three messaging apps. A high-quality JPG around 90 is usually indistinguishable from PNG for photographic frames at store size, while PNG stays the safer choice for UI text and flat colour. If a PNG is unusually large, convert it to JPG at high quality instead of dropping the PNG palette hard — text and thin interface lines are the first things to break under aggressive palette reduction.

Keep an untouched original for every approved screenshot. When you update the store page months later, re-editing the uploaded copy means stacking a second round of compression on the first.

Pre-Upload Checklist

  • At least five screenshots, each at 1920 × 1080 or larger and exactly 16:9.
  • No promotional text, prices, URLs, QR codes, review scores or watermarks.
  • No chat, FPS counters, debug labels or developer consoles.
  • The first frame is readable as a small thumbnail.
  • Every frame is sharp at 100% and free of bars.
  • The gallery shows different systems and environments, not five versions of one scene.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many screenshots does Steam require?

Steamworks requires at least five screenshots before a store page can go live. Upload six to eight if you can, because the gallery benefits from a strong opening frame plus enough variety to show different systems and environments.

Can Steam screenshots be 2560×1440 or 4K?

Yes. Anything at or above 1920×1080 is accepted as long as the aspect ratio stays 16:9. A 2560×1440 or 3840×2160 capture downscaled to 1080p often looks cleaner than a native 1080p capture.

Can I add text or a logo to a Steam screenshot?

Avoid promotional overlays. Text callouts, prices, discount badges, URLs, QR codes and review scores are commonly flagged during store review. Keep the frame as gameplay and put marketing copy on the capsule or in the description instead.

Do Steam screenshots have to be 16:9?

Yes for the store gallery. Capture at a native 16:9 resolution such as 1920×1080, 2560×1440 or 3840×2160. Ultrawide or 16:10 captures appear with bars or get cropped, which looks unfinished and can be rejected.
