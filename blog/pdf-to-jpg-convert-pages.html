How to Convert PDF Pages to JPG or PNG Images

Published on Nov 10, 2026 · 4 min read

PDFs are great for documents and terrible for sharing. Discord won't preview them, most social platforms reject them outright, and you can't drop one into a video timeline or an image editor. Converting pages to JPG or PNG solves all of that — and with a browser-based converter, even confidential documents stay on your own machine.

When You'd Actually Do This

  • Slides and lecture decks: turn each page into an image for note apps like Notion or OneNote.
  • Rulebook tables and maps: keep a quick-reference JPG on your phone for game night.
  • Social posts: share one page of a zine, menu, or schedule as an image carousel.
  • Thumbnails and previews: generate a cover image from page one of an ebook or report.
  • Annotation: pull a page into any image editor to mark it up with arrows and highlights.

Step 1: Open the Converter

Visit the PDF to image converter. It renders pages locally using PDF.js — the same engine Firefox uses — so nothing is uploaded and there's no queue.

Step 2: Upload Your PDF

Drag the file in. Scanned PDFs work fine: every page is rendered exactly as it appears, whether it contains real text or a flat scan.

Step 3: Choose PNG or JPG

  • PNG — the right default for text, tables, and line art. Sharp edges, zero compression smearing, larger files.
  • JPG — exported at 95% quality. Much smaller, ideal for photo-heavy pages where you'll never zoom into 8pt text.

Unsure? Convert one page both ways and compare — it takes seconds.

Step 4: Convert and Download

Each page renders at 2× scale — double the PDF's native dimensions — so small text stays crisp on retina screens and when zooming. Thumbnails appear as pages finish. Save individual pages, or click Download All to get everything in one ZIP archive (browsers block one click from downloading dozens of files, hence the ZIP).

Tips for Best Results

  • Split first if you only need a few pages. Converting a 300-page PDF takes a while; use the PDF splitter to extract the section first, then convert just that.
  • JPG for sharing, PNG for archiving. If the image will be edited or annotated later, PNG's losslessness saves you from generation-loss mush.
  • Still too big? Run the exported images through the image compressor before posting.

Why 2× Rendering Matters

PDF pages are vector-based — text is drawn from font outlines, not pixels — so a page can be rendered at any resolution. This converter renders at double the page's native size, which means the exported images hold up on high-DPI phone screens and stay readable when someone zooms into a dense table. Render once at 2× and you can always downscale later; render too small and the detail is gone for good.

How It Compares to Screenshots

You could screenshot each PDF page — but you get whatever fits on your screen at whatever resolution your monitor has, plus window chrome if you're sloppy. Rendering converts the actual page content at a consistent 2× resolution, edge to edge, every time.
