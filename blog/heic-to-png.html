HEIC to PNG: Convert iPhone Photos Without Uploading Them

Published on Nov 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Open the HEIC converter, drop your photos in, pick PNG as the output format, and download the results. The whole conversion runs inside your browser, so the photos never leave your device. The part worth understanding before you start is why the file gets ten times bigger and whether PNG is actually the right target for the photo you are holding.

HEIC and PNG Side by Side

The size jump is the number that surprises people. It is not a bug in the converter: PNG has to record every pixel individually, while HEIC throws away information the eye is unlikely to notice and then compresses what is left. A 12-megapixel image has twelve million pixels, and PNG writes all of them.

Why Browsers Cannot Open HEIC Directly

HEIC is a container based on HEIF and encoded with HEVC, the video codec. Only Safari ships a decoder on desktop and mobile; Chrome, Firefox and Edge do not, which is why a .heic file dragged into a browser tab or attached to a web form simply fails. This is also why so many upload fields reject HEIC even when they accept JPG without complaint.

Converting to PNG solves the compatibility problem completely, because PNG has been supported everywhere for decades. Converting to JPG also solves it and keeps the file small. The choice between them is about what happens next to the image, not about compatibility.

When PNG Is the Right Target

  • You are going to edit it repeatedly. Every JPG save re-compresses and discards a little more detail. PNG does not, so a PNG master survives many rounds of cropping and retouching.
  • You need to composite it. Cutting a subject out of a photo, placing it on a background, or building a thumbnail in a design tool all work better from a lossless source.
  • It is not really a photo. iPhone screenshots, memes, UI captures and flat graphics compress badly as JPG and pick up visible ringing around text. PNG keeps hard edges hard.
  • You want an archival copy. If the original HEIC is the only copy you have, a PNG master is a safer long-term bet than another lossy format.

When JPG Is the Better Choice

If the goal is to send the photo to Discord, attach it to an email, or upload it to a site with a size cap, PNG will fight you. A 30 MB PNG fails most upload limits and takes noticeably longer to share. In that case convert to JPG instead — same converter, different output setting — and the typical 12-megapixel photo lands around 2–4 MB at high quality.

A middle path that works well: keep a PNG master, then produce a JPG copy for anything that leaves your machine. You get the editing headroom and the practical file size.

Converting a Batch of Photos

  1. Get the files off the phone. On macOS you can drag them straight out of Photos or Finder; on Windows, plug the phone in and copy the DCIM folder, or use iCloud for Windows. Empty Airdrop to a Mac also works and keeps the original HEIC.
  2. Drop them all at once. The HEIC to JPG/PNG converter accepts multiple files per drop. It queues them and converts as a batch rather than one dialog per photo.
  3. Set the output format to PNG. If the panel offers a quality control it only affects the JPG path; PNG ignores it because lossless has no quality dial.
  4. Download individually or all at once. Each result has its own download button, and Download All collects the batch so you are not clicking twenty times.

The File Size Problem, and How to Fix It

Twenty 12-megapixel photos as PNG can easily total 500 MB. If you only needed PNG for one editing pass, the sensible sequence is convert, edit, then compress the final result. The image compressor can bring a PNG down by reducing its colour palette — effective on screenshots and flat art, less so on photographic noise.

If you need PNG specifically for a web page, check whether the site actually requires PNG. Many accept WebP, which is dramatically smaller than PNG at the same visual quality, and the format converter handles that conversion in one step.

Live Photos and Multi-Frame Files

A Live Photo is a still image plus a short video track stored together. A converter only has a still frame to work with, so the output is a single PNG representing the key frame — the motion part is dropped. That is expected behaviour, not data corruption, but it does mean a Live Photo converted to PNG cannot be turned back into a Live Photo later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert HEIC to PNG?

Open the HEIC to JPG converter, drop your .heic or .heif files into the upload area, choose PNG as the output format, and the browser decodes and re-encodes each photo. Download them one by one or use Download All. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

Why is the PNG file so much larger than the HEIC original?

HEIC uses HEVC compression, which is roughly twice as efficient as JPEG, while PNG is lossless and stores every pixel. A 12-megapixel photo that is 2–3 MB as HEIC commonly becomes 20–40 MB as PNG. That is expected, not a sign of a broken conversion.

Does converting to PNG restore lost detail?

No. HEIC is a lossy format, so detail removed during compression is already gone. Converting to PNG preserves exactly what remains without adding further loss, which matters when you plan to edit the image repeatedly.

Should I choose PNG or JPG for iPhone photos?

Choose JPG when the photo is going straight to a chat app, a forum or a website and you need a small file. Choose PNG when you intend to edit the image, layer it in a design tool, or keep it as an archival copy, and accept the larger size.

Can I convert several HEIC files at once?

Yes. Drop multiple files at once and the tool converts them as a batch, then offers a separate download for each result plus a Download All option. Because decoding happens on your device, there is no upload queue and no per-file limit tied to an account.
