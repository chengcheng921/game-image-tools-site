PNG vs JPG vs WebP — Which Format Should You Choose?

Each format wins somewhere. PNG is lossless and supports transparency, so it's right for screenshots with crisp UI text, logos, and anything you'll edit again — but files are big. JPEG is the universal photo format: small files, no transparency, and visible artifacts if you push quality too low. WebP is the modern middle ground, typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality, with transparency support — and every current browser, Discord, and most CMSs accept it. For sharing game screenshots, WebP at quality 80–90 is hard to beat; for archiving, keep the PNG.

Why Convert Instead of Just Renaming the File?

Changing a file extension doesn't change the data inside — a renamed PNG is still a PNG, and strict apps will reject it. Real conversion re-encodes the image, which is what actually shrinks the file or adds compatibility with software that refuses certain formats.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to transparency when I convert PNG to JPEG?

JPEG doesn't support transparency, so transparent areas are flattened onto a solid background. Choose WebP or PNG output if you need to keep the alpha channel.

Why is my converted file bigger than the original?

Converting a compressed format (like JPEG) to a lossless one (PNG) stores every pixel exactly, which takes more space. To shrink files, convert toward JPEG or WebP instead.

Are my images uploaded for conversion?

No — conversion runs locally in your browser with the Canvas API. Free, no signup, and your files never leave your device.
